Eleven mistakes that make tofu taste bad

A diagnostic list. Find the symptom you got, read the cause, apply the fix. Most bad tofu is one of these.

1. You bought the wrong block

Symptom: it fell apart in the pan, or turned to scrambled fragments.

Silken and soft tofu will never crisp. They are excellent in soup, sauces and desserts and hopeless in a hot pan. If you cannot remember what you bought and the block collapsed, this was almost certainly it.

Fix: buy extra-firm for anything involving browning. The types guide covers which block does which job.

2. You skipped pressing

Symptom: pale, soggy, no crust, and the pan filled with liquid.

A sixteen ounce block holds several tablespoons of water. That water boils off before anything can brown, dropping the pan temperature while it does. This is the most common single failure.

Fix: twenty to thirty minutes under weight. The pressing guide has four methods including a fifteen minute version.

3. You crowded the pan

Symptom: some pieces browned, most stayed pale and soft.

Pieces touching each other trap steam between them. The pan fills with moisture, the surface temperature collapses, and the batch steams instead of frying. This is the mistake people repeat most often after fixing the first two.

Fix: single layer, visible gaps, two batches if needed. The second batch cooks faster because the pan is already hot.

4. The heat was too low

Symptom: greasy, rubbery, oil-soaked tofu.

At low heat the tofu sits in oil and absorbs it rather than searing against it. Low and slow is a technique for connective tissue. Tofu has none, so there is nothing for the time to do except dry the interior out.

Fix: medium-high, oil shimmering before the tofu goes in. See the crisping guide.

5. You moved it too early

Symptom: it stuck, tore, and left its surface in the pan.

Tofu sticks at first and releases on its own once a crust forms. A piece that will not lift is telling you it is not ready. Prying it up removes the crust and guarantees it sticks again on the next side.

Fix: three to four minutes undisturbed on the first side. If it resists the spatula, wait.

6. You used too little oil

Symptom: patchy browning, sticking, and dry spots.

A thin film beads up and leaves parts of each piece with no contact. Two to three tablespoons for one block in a twelve inch pan is the baseline. Well-pressed tofu absorbs far less of it than you would expect, because most stays in the pan.

Fix: more oil, and one with a high smoke point. Olive oil and toasted sesame oil both burn at these temperatures.

7. You cut the pieces too small

Symptom: dry, hollow, hard little cubes with nothing inside.

Small pieces overcook through to the centre before the outside finishes browning. There is no tender interior left by the time the crust arrives.

Fix: three quarter inch cubes or half inch slabs, cut evenly. Mixed sizes in one pan means some burn while others stay pale.

8. You added the sauce too early

Symptom: it was crisp, then it was not.

A starch crust survives liquid for under a minute before it rehydrates. Sauce added at the start of cooking, or simmered with the tofu, removes the texture you just spent fifteen minutes building.

Fix: sauce at the end, off high heat, tossed briefly. For maximum crunch, combine on the plate rather than in the pan.

9. You under-seasoned it

Symptom: the texture was right and it still tasted of nothing.

Tofu brings no salt, no fat and no browning compounds of its own. A seasoning level that would be aggressive on chicken lands as mild here. This is the adjustment people resist longest.

Fix: salt at three points. In the starch coating, in the sauce, and on the hot tofu as it leaves the pan. The seasoning guide has a formula that works on almost anything.

10. You marinated instead of changing the tofu

Symptom: flavour on the outside, nothing in the middle, even after hours.

Fresh tofu is already saturated, so marinade has nowhere to travel. Four hours achieves barely more than fifteen minutes. Marinating also adds water back to a block you just pressed, which costs you the crust.

Fix: freeze and thaw the block, which opens channels the marinade can actually enter. The freezing guide covers it. Or skip marinating and sauce after cooking.

11. You expected it to be meat

Symptom: technically fine, still disappointing.

Judged as a chicken substitute, tofu loses. It has a different texture, a different bite and a different relationship with sauce. Most of the disappointment people report is a comparison problem rather than a cooking problem.

Fix: judge it on its own terms, as a firm savoury protein that carries sauce unusually well and cooks in fifteen minutes. The page on this goes further.

If you can only fix two things: press the block, and stop crowding the pan. Those two account for the large majority of bad results. Everything else on this list is a refinement on top of them, and none of the refinements will rescue a wet block cooked in a full pan.

What to do next

Cook basic crispy tofu with nothing on it but salt and eat a piece plain. That isolates the technique from the recipe. If the plain piece is good, every recipe in the recipe section will work. If it is not, the problem is on this page, and it is probably number two or number three.
