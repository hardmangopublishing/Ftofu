Why most people hate tofu

It is almost never the tofu. It is what happened to it before it reached the plate.

1

It was full of water

Tofu is packed in liquid. Skip the pressing step and that water boils off in the pan, steaming the block instead of browning it. Soggy is a process failure, not a property of the food.

How to press it →
2

It was barely seasoned

Tofu contributes texture and protein. Flavor is your job. Seasoned the way you would season a potato, it tastes like almost nothing, and everyone blames the tofu.

Fix the flavor →
3

It was cooked too gently

Steamed or simmered tofu stays soft all the way through. Crisp edges need real heat, real fat and enough space in the pan for moisture to leave.

Get it crispy →
4

It was asked to be meat

Tofu loses that comparison every time. Treated as its own thing, a firm savory protein that carries sauce well, it stops disappointing people.

Nobody here is trying to convert you

You can eat tofu once a month or twice a week. You can eat a steak tomorrow. This site exists to make the tofu you do eat worth eating, which is a smaller and much more achievable goal than changing your diet.

Start with the four fixes

The four fixes that change everything

Learn these once and every recipe on this site becomes easier.

1

Press out the water

Twenty minutes under a heavy pan removes enough liquid to change how the block behaves in heat. This single step solves the majority of texture complaints.

The pressing guide →
2

Freeze, then thaw

Ice crystals open channels through the block. Thawed and squeezed, the tofu turns chewy and porous, and marinade finally gets past the surface.

The freezing method →
3

High heat and some fat

A hot pan, enough oil to make real contact, and the patience to leave the pieces alone until a crust forms. Rushing this is why tofu sticks and tears.

The crisping guide →
4

Season past the point of comfort

Salt, soy sauce, acid, aromatics, a little sweetness. Tofu absorbs seasoning without tasting salty the way meat does, so the usual restraint works against you.

The seasoning guide →

A dedicated press is faster and tidier and applies even pressure. A cast iron skillet on a towel does the same job for free, just less neatly.
