The 4 Fixes

These techniques solve the majority of complaints people have about tofu. Master them once and everything improves.

1. Press the water out

Tofu is packed in water. If you don’t remove a good portion of it, you get steamed, soggy results no matter what else you do.

How to do it well:

  1. Remove tofu from the package and drain.
  2. Wrap in a clean kitchen towel or paper towels.
  3. Place on a plate and put something heavy on top (cast iron pan, books, or a tofu press).
  4. Let sit 15–30 minutes (or longer for firmer results).
  5. Pat dry again before cooking.

Quick version: If you’re short on time, wrap and microwave for 1–2 minutes, then press briefly. Not perfect, but better than nothing.

Recommended tool: A dedicated tofu press makes this step much easier and more consistent.

2. Freeze, then thaw (the texture upgrade)

This is the secret many people never learn. Freezing changes the structure of the tofu. When it thaws, it becomes more porous and chewy — much closer to a meaty texture and far better at absorbing marinade.

How to do it:

  1. Press the tofu first (optional but recommended).
  2. Place the block in a freezer-safe container or bag.
  3. Freeze solid (overnight is ideal).
  4. Thaw in the fridge or under cool running water.
  5. Squeeze out the extra water that releases. The tofu will look darker and have a spongy texture — this is what you want.

You can freeze multiple blocks at once so you always have “ready to absorb flavor” tofu on hand.

3. Use high heat and some fat

Low-and-slow or steaming keeps tofu soft and watery. High heat creates the crispy edges that make people change their minds.

Best methods:

  • Pan-fry: Medium-high heat, enough oil to coat the pan well. Don’t move the pieces too early — let a crust form.
  • Air fryer: 375–400°F (190–200°C), lightly oiled, 12–18 minutes depending on size, shaking halfway.
  • Oven: 400–425°F on a well-oiled or parchment-lined sheet, flipping once.

Coat the tofu lightly in cornstarch, arrowroot, or a bit of flour after seasoning for even better crispiness.

Helpful tools:
• Air fryer – makes consistently crispy tofu with less oil
• Cornstarch or arrowroot – for extra crunch
• A good non-stick or cast iron pan

4. Season aggressively

Tofu is mild by nature. That is a feature, not a bug — but only if you treat it like a flavor sponge.

Rules of thumb:

  • Salt it. Then salt it a little more than you think.
  • Use soy sauce, tamari, or coconut aminos as a base for almost every marinade.
  • Add acid (rice vinegar, lime, lemon) for brightness.
  • Add aromatics: garlic, ginger, chili, smoked paprika, cumin, etc.
  • Let it sit in the marinade at least 15–20 minutes (longer is better, especially after freezing).

A simple winning formula: soy sauce + oil + garlic + something sweet (maple or a pinch of sugar) + chili or smoked paprika.

Flavor boosters worth having:
• Chili garlic sauce (Huy Fong style)
• Smoked paprika
• Toasted sesame oil
• Good soy sauce or tamari
