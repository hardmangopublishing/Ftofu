Gear that earns its space

Four things make tofu meaningfully easier and one of them is free. Everything else on this page is optional, and we say which is which.

The short version

You need a way to remove water, a pan that browns, a source of high dry heat, and about nine pantry items. That is the whole list. You almost certainly own two of those already.

The honest position on tofu presses is that they are a convenience purchase rather than a requirement. A heavy skillet on a towel removes a similar amount of water. What a press buys you is even pressure, a tidier counter and one less thing to think about, which for some people is worth twenty dollars and for others is not.

Removing the water

Pressing is the highest-value step in tofu cooking, so it is worth understanding the options before spending anything. Screw presses apply the most force and take the longest. Spring and plate presses are faster to set up and easier to clean. Weighted towel methods cost nothing and work fine if you are willing to check on them.

Applying the heat

Tofu browns through direct contact with a hot surface or through fast-moving hot air. Pans do the first, air fryers and ovens do the second. Each has a case.

A heavy pan gives the deepest crust and the most control, and it is the only method that produces a proper fond for a pan sauce. It also demands that you stand there and turn things. Cast iron and carbon steel both hold heat well enough to stop the tofu from cooling the surface when it goes in, which is exactly what lighter nonstick pans fail to do.

An air fryer is the most forgiving option and uses the least oil. It also handles the awkward part of tofu cooking, which is the fifteen minutes of turning pieces one at a time. If you already own one, use it before buying anything else.

An oven and a sheet pan handle volume better than either, which matters if you are cooking two or three blocks for the week. Slower, less crisp, entirely hands-off.

The pantry

This is where the flavor actually lives, and it is the cheapest part of the list. Soy sauce or tamari, a starch for the crust, a chili paste, a vinegar, toasted sesame oil, smoked paprika, garlic in some form, something sweet, and salt you are willing to use generously.

Nine items, most of them under ten dollars, and they cover every recipe on this site with room left over.

A tray, which is not really gear at all

The cheapest thing on this page and arguably the most useful. An ice cube tray full of frozen flavour concentrate turns seasoning from a decision into a scoop. One cube is portioned to season one block of tofu, and a batch made once on a Sunday lasts for months.

Freezer flavour cubes: a sauce base system →

Skip buying anything if: you own a heavy skillet, a clean dish towel and a bottle of soy sauce. That is a working tofu setup. Press with the skillet, cook in the skillet, season with the soy sauce. Cook it that way three or four times before deciding whether any of the rest is worth money to you. Buying gear before you have a method is how kitchen cupboards fill up with things nobody uses.

What is genuinely not worth it

Tofu-specific cubing gadgets, marinade injectors and vacuum marinating containers all address problems that pressing and freezing solve more effectively and for less money. Silicone tofu molds are for people making tofu from soybeans, which is a different hobby.

Nonstick sprays are worth avoiding on pans you care about, and they do not deliver enough fat for good browning anyway. Use oil from a bottle.

The one purchase most people underrate is a wider pan. Crowding ruins more tofu than any missing tool, and a twelve inch skillet fixes it permanently.
