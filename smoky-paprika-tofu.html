Smoky paprika tofu

A thick spice paste instead of a wet marinade, so the seasoning clings and browns rather than running off. Good hot, better cold the next day.

Prep25 min
Cook30 min
Serves3
Keeps4 days

This is the meal prep recipe. It is built around a paste rather than a marinade, which is the whole trick: a thin liquid runs off a dense block and takes its flavour with it, while a paste stays where you put it and dries into a coating in the oven.

The result holds up cold, which almost nothing else on this site does. Straight from the container into a bowl, a wrap or a salad, three days later, still worth eating.

Smoked paprika is Spanish, from La Vera in Extremadura, where the peppers are dried over oak smoke rather than in the sun. That smoke is doing all the heavy lifting here. Ordinary sweet paprika is not a substitute and the recipe falls flat without it.

Ingredients

  • 1 block (14 to 16 oz) extra-firm tofu, pressed 20 to 30 minutes, or frozen and thawed for a chewier result
  • 1.5 tablespoons olive oil or avocado oil
  • 1.5 teaspoons smoked paprika
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • Half a teaspoon of onion powder
  • 3/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • Half a teaspoon of black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon maple syrup
  • 1 teaspoon sherry vinegar or lemon juice, added after cooking

Garlic and onion powder rather than fresh, deliberately. Fresh garlic burns at roasting temperature and the powders dissolve into the paste and coat evenly, which is what you want from a dry-style rub.

Method

  1. Press and cut. Twenty to thirty minutes under weight, then three quarter inch cubes. If the block was frozen and thawed, tear it into irregular pieces instead. The ragged edges crisp better and the freezing guide explains why the texture changes.
  2. Make the paste. Stir the oil, smoked paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, salt, pepper, soy sauce and maple syrup together. It should coat a spoon rather than pour off it. If it looks thin, add another quarter teaspoon of paprika.
  3. Coat. Toss the tofu through the paste until every face is covered and the pieces look dark red. Let it sit fifteen minutes if you have the time, which helps but is not essential.
  4. Cook. Roast at 425F on an oiled sheet pan for 25 to 30 minutes, turning once. Or air fry at 390F for 15 to 18 minutes. Or pan fry over medium-high for about 12 minutes.
  5. Finish with acid. Vinegar or lemon straight out of the pan while the tofu is hot. This is the step people skip and it is the difference between good and flat.

No cornstarch here. The paste itself forms the coating, and starch on top of it produces a thick pasty layer rather than a crust.

Why the paste beats a marinade

Fresh tofu is already saturated with water, so a thin marinade has nowhere to go and mostly ends up in the bottom of the bowl. Sitting it for four hours changes almost nothing, as the seasoning guide sets out in detail.

A paste sidesteps the problem entirely by accepting that flavour lives on the surface and putting far more of it there. Oil carries the fat-soluble compounds in paprika, salt and soy provide the savoury base, and the small amount of sugar helps the exterior brown.

The same principle drives every rub-based cooking tradition. It works particularly well on tofu because tofu has more surface relative to its volume than most proteins once it is cut.

Meal prep notes

Double the batch and use two sheet pans rather than crowding one. Steam from a full pan is what stops tofu browning, and the oven guide covers pan spacing.

Cool it fully on the pan before it goes into a container. Sealed warm, condensation forms and the coating goes soft by morning.

Four days refrigerated. It goes into grain bowls, wraps, salads, tacos, scrambled eggs, or onto toast with avocado. Cold it reads almost like a cured sausage in texture, which is an unexpected selling point with people who claim to dislike tofu.

What this recipe uses:
  • Smoked paprika, the one ingredient with no substitute here
  • A heavy half sheet pan
  • Storage containers

Variations

Add half a teaspoon of cumin and a pinch of cayenne and it moves toward a chorizo-adjacent flavour, which is excellent crumbled rather than cubed. Swap the paprika for harissa and it goes North African. Swap it for chipotle in adobo and it goes Mexican.

All three are worth freezing as a base, since a paste this thick is exactly what the freezer cube system is designed for.

Skip this one if: you want a hard crisp crust tonight. The paste browns and firms but it never gets properly crunchy, because there is no starch and the coating holds a little moisture. For crunch, cook basic crispy tofu and season it after. This recipe is optimised for keeping, not for the first ten minutes.
